A Short History of the Hot Hatch

The hot hatch began in the 1970s when engineers gave compact hatchbacks stronger engines and firmer suspension. The first Volkswagen Golf GTI proved that a practical car could also be exciting to drive.

The 1980s and 1990s brought rivals from Peugeot, Renault and Ford, each with its own character. Light weight, front-wheel drive and playful handling defined the formula.

Today's hot hatches add turbochargers, clever differentials and advanced electronics. Electric versions are arriving now, showing the idea of a small, quick, affordable car is still alive.
