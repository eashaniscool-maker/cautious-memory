How Car Engines Work, Explained Simply

Most petrol and diesel cars use a four-stroke cycle: intake, compression, power and exhaust. Each piston moves up and down inside a cylinder, turning fuel into motion.

A crankshaft converts that up-and-down motion into rotation, which the gearbox sends to the wheels. A turbocharger uses exhaust gas to force extra air in, making more power from a smaller engine.

Oil reduces friction between moving parts and coolant carries heat away. Skipping either is the fastest way to destroy an engine, which is why regular checks matter.
